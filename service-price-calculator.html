How the calculator works

Most pros set prices by looking at what others charge. That tells you what clients expect, but not whether the price actually pays your bills. This calculator works from your side of the chair:

  1. Your yearly target = income goal + business costs × 12.
  2. Client hours per year = hours per week × how full your book is × weeks you work.
  3. Cost per client hour = yearly target ÷ client hours.
  4. Minimum price = cost per client hour × service time + products.

The suggested price is rounded up to the nearest 5, because that's how most price lists look — and a few extra dollars per client cover the things you forgot to count.

What counts as business costs

  • Rent, booth rent or suite rent
  • Insurance, licences and memberships
  • Software, booking tools and card payment fees
  • Tools and equipment (spread the cost over a year)
  • Education and courses
  • Laundry, cleaning, towels and disposables you don't count per service
  • Marketing, website and phone

Products you use on a specific service — color, gel, lashes, wax — go into Products used per service instead, so expensive services carry their own cost.

Common pricing mistakes

  • Counting only the service time. Consultation, setup, cleanup and the gap between clients are part of the service. Include them in the minutes.
  • Assuming a full book. Almost nobody is booked 100% of the time. Cancellations, no-shows and quiet weeks are why the "how full" number matters.
  • Forgetting holidays and sick days. If you take 6 weeks off a year, your prices have to cover them.
  • Copying the cheapest salon nearby. If their costs or income goal are lower than yours, their prices won't work for you.
  • Never raising prices. Your costs go up every year. Check your numbers at least once a year — and use these price increase message templates to tell clients.
