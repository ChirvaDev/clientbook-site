How to use it

  1. At the first appointment, ask the client to fill in page 1 — or go through it together during the consultation — and sign it.
  2. After every visit, add a row to the visit log while it's fresh: the service, exact products or formula, the price and one note for next time. Not sure what to charge? Try the service price calculator.
  3. Before the next visit, read the card. Thirty seconds is enough to remember the formula, the allergy and what they told you about their trip.
  4. Update page 1 when something changes — a new phone number, a new allergy, a reaction to a product.

What to write for your service

  • Hair: color formulas with brand, shade, developer and timing; cut length and guard numbers; how they style it at home.
  • Nails: shape, length, system (gel, acrylic, dip) and brand, colors they've had, any reactions to gel or primer.
  • Lashes: curl, thickness and lengths by zone, adhesive used, patch test, retention between fills.
  • Brows & waxing: shape and tint, wax type, skin care or medication that makes skin more sensitive.
  • Skin care: skin type, products and peels used, how the skin reacted.
  • Massage: pressure, areas to focus on or avoid, injuries, oils or lotions to skip.

Keep client records private

A client card holds personal details and sometimes health information. Store paper cards in a closed box or drawer, not on the front desk, and only write down what you actually need. In the UK and EU, client details count as personal data under GDPR, so the same applies to photos and notes on your phone.

Paper card or app?

Paper is free, simple and works without a phone. It gets harder as your client list grows: cards get lost, they're not with you when a client texts, and you can't search them. That's why many pros move their cards to an app once they have more than a few dozen regulars.
